# Can Personality Traits Predict Musical Style Preferences? A Meta-Analysis

**Authors:** Thomas Schäfer and Claudia Mehlhorn
**Year:** 2017
**Journal:** Personality and Individual Differences, Volume 116, Pages 265–273
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.paid.2017.04.061
**Note:** Research summary compiled from publicly available abstracts, citations, and secondary sources. Not a reproduction of the original paper.

## Research Question

Can Big Five personality traits reliably predict which musical styles a person prefers? While individual studies since Rentfrow and Gosling (2003) have reported correlations between personality and musical taste, results have been inconsistent. This meta-analysis synthesizes approximately 30 studies to determine the true average effect sizes.

## Methodology

### Personality Framework
Six personality variables were used because they appeared most frequently across the included studies:
- **Openness to Experience:** Intellectual curiosity, creativity, preference for novelty
- **Conscientiousness:** Organization, dependability, self-discipline
- **Extraversion:** Sociability, assertiveness, positive emotionality
- **Agreeableness:** Cooperativeness, trust, empathy
- **Neuroticism:** Emotional instability, anxiety, moodiness
- **Sensation Seeking:** Pursuit of novel, intense experiences and stimulation

### Music Preference Framework: MUSIC Model
The five-dimensional MUSIC model (Rentfrow, Goldberg, & Levitin, 2011) was used because it was the most common framework across the reviewed studies. It classifies preferences by acoustic and emotional qualities rather than genre labels:
- **Mellow:** Romantic, relaxing, slow. Soft rock, R&B, smooth jazz.
- **Unpretentious:** Uncomplicated, acoustic, sincere. Country, folk, singer-songwriter.
- **Sophisticated:** Complex, dynamic, cerebral. Classical, opera, jazz, world music.
- **Intense:** Loud, aggressive, energetic. Rock, punk, heavy metal, alternative.
- **Contemporary:** Rhythmic, upbeat, electronic. Rap, hip-hop, electronica, dance, pop.

### Analysis Design
6 personality variables × 5 music dimensions = 30 sub-analyses. For each combination, weighted average correlation coefficients were calculated, giving larger studies proportionally more influence.

## Key Results

### Overall Finding
All 30 weighted average correlation coefficients were very small. The overall average across all combinations was r = 0.058, indicating a near-zero relationship. Only 6 of 30 coefficients exceeded |r| ≥ 0.1 (the conventional threshold for a small effect in psychology).

### The Six Notable Correlations
The meaningful effects were concentrated around Openness, with smaller contributions from Extraversion and Sensation Seeking:

1. **Openness → Sophisticated (r ≈ .15–.21):** The strongest and most replicated finding in the entire field. Open individuals consistently prefer complex music like classical, jazz, and world music. This held across cultures and methods.
2. **Openness → Intense (r ≈ .10–.15):** Open individuals also enjoy aggressive music like rock and metal, reflecting the novelty-seeking aspect of Openness rather than aesthetic refinement.
3. **Openness → Mellow (r ≈ .10):** A weaker tendency for open individuals to appreciate soft, relaxing music, likely reflecting broad aesthetic appreciation.
4. **Extraversion → Contemporary (r ≈ .10–.13):** Extraverts prefer rhythmic, danceable music, which makes sense given that such music often accompanies social activities.
5. **Extraversion → Unpretentious (r ≈ .10):** A weaker tendency for extraverts to enjoy sincere, uncomplicated music.
6. **Sensation Seeking → Intense (r ≈ .10–.15):** High sensation seekers prefer loud, aggressive music, consistent with their arousal-seeking tendencies.

### Non-Significant Traits
- **Conscientiousness:** No consistent relationship with any music dimension.
- **Agreeableness:** No consistent relationship with any music dimension.
- **Neuroticism:** No consistent relationship, despite theoretical expectations. Individual studies found links to Intense or Mellow music, but these did not replicate in aggregate.

### Effect Size Context
Even the strongest correlation (Openness → Sophisticated at r ≈ .20) explains only about 4% of variance. For most personality-music combinations, explained variance is below 1%.

## Alternative Explanation: Musical Functions

The authors propose that personality may relate more to WHY people listen to music than to WHAT they listen to. The functions of music listening include:
- **Mood regulation:** Using music to manage emotional states
- **Identity expression:** Music as a signal of who you are
- **Social bonding:** Music as a tool for connection and belonging
- **Cognitive stimulation:** Music for focus, creativity, or intellectual engagement
- **Arousal management:** Music to increase or decrease physiological arousal

An extravert playing pop at a party and an introvert playing ambient music alone may both be using music for mood regulation — same function, different style. This would explain why style preferences correlate weakly with personality while the underlying motivations might correlate strongly.

## Criticisms and Later Developments

Devenport and North (2019) challenged the conclusions by showing that personality ASPECTS (subdivisions of the Big Five, e.g. splitting Openness into Intellect and Aesthetic Sensitivity) are substantially better predictors than the broad domains (R² = .20–.28 vs. .12–.23 for three of four music dimensions).

Subsequent studies using actual streaming data from Spotify and machine learning models (e.g., Sust et al., 2023; Anderson et al., 2021) found stronger predictive relationships, particularly when combining multiple behavioral signals rather than relying on self-reported genre preferences alone.

## Implications for Music-Based Personality Prediction Systems

- **Openness is the reliable signal.** It is the only trait where music data consistently adds meaningful predictive value.
- **Conscientiousness, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism predictions should carry low confidence** when based on music data alone.
- **Going beyond genres improves prediction.** Listening diversity, repeat behavior, temporal patterns, and mainstream-vs-niche orientation provide additional signal that simple genre preferences do not capture.
- **Transparency matters.** Overstating prediction accuracy is both scientifically unsupported and commercially risky, especially in professional (HR/B2B) contexts.
- **Musical functions are a promising direction.** Systems that can capture how people use music (not just what they play) may achieve substantially better personality prediction.
